# CreditCoach: 1:1 User Interview Questionnaire

*Additional research task (outside the Week 1 plan) · Interviewer: Sudip · 4–6 participants, aged 21–42, working at multinational companies · About 12–15 minutes*

---

## Before You Start (read aloud)

> "Thanks for your time. This takes about 12–15 minutes and has 25 multiple-choice questions about credit scores, cards, loans, and how you split your salary. There are no right or wrong answers, and 'I don't know' is always a fine answer. We don't record your name or any exact amounts, only ranges. You can skip any question. Your answers help us design a tool that explains credit scores in plain language. OK to begin?"

**Participant ID:** P___ (use P1–P6, never a name) **Date:** ________

**Interviewer tips**
- Read the options aloud and circle one answer, unless the question says *select all that apply*.
- Don't correct wrong answers during the interview. Wait until the end (see "After the Interview").
- Salary questions use **percentage of monthly take-home pay**, never amounts, so they work in any country and currency.

## Section B: Understanding Your Credit Score (10 questions)

**4. How often do you check your credit score?**
- a) Never
- b) Once a year or less
- c) Every few months
- d) Monthly or more often

**5. Where do you mostly learn about credit scores?**
- a) Social media or YouTube
- b) My bank's or card issuer's app or website
- c) Friends, family, or colleagues
- d) Search engines and blogs
- e) I don't really look it up

**6. Which best describes your current credit score?**
- a) I don't know it
- b) Below average / poor
- c) Average / fair
- d) Good
- e) Excellent

**7. Which of these has the biggest effect on most credit scores?**
- a) Your salary
- b) Paying bills on time
- c) How many bank accounts you have
- d) Your age
- e) I don't know

**8. What does "credit utilization" mean?**
- a) How many credit cards you have
- b) The share of your credit card limit you're currently using
- c) How often you swipe your card
- d) Your total spending in a year
- e) I don't know

**9. Above roughly what level of utilization do scores commonly start to drop?**
- a) Above 10%
- b) Above 30%
- c) Above 70%
- d) Only when the card is maxed out (100%)
- e) I don't know

**10. What usually happens to your score when you apply for a new credit card or loan?**
- a) Nothing happens
- b) A small, temporary dip
- c) A large, permanent drop
- d) It goes up
- e) I don't know

**11. What might happen if you close your oldest credit card?**
- a) Your score goes up because you have less credit
- b) Nothing, because closed cards don't count
- c) Your score may go down because your credit history looks shorter
- d) I don't know

**12. A company offers to raise your credit score by 100 points within 30 days, for a fee paid upfront. What is this most likely to be?**
- a) A good deal if you can afford the fee
- b) A standard service that most banks offer
- c) A red flag for a possible scam
- d) I don't know

**13. Have you ever seen your credit score drop and not known why?**
- a) Yes, and I never found out why
- b) Yes, but I figured out the reason
- c) No, it has never dropped as far as I know
- d) I've never checked closely enough to know

### Interviewer answer key: credit knowledge score (Q7–Q12)

Grounded in `credit_score_factors_guide.pdf`. Give 1 point per correct answer; "I don't know" scores 0.

|---|---|---|

| 8 | b) Share of your card limit you're using | §2 Balance divided by limit |

**Knowledge score bands:** 0–2 = low · 3–4 = partial · 5–6 = strong
