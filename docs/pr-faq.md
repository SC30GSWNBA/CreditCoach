## Press Release

### CreditCoach Launches: Finally, Someone Can Explain Why Your Credit Score Dropped

**A plain-language credit coach for first-time borrowers explains your score using your own account data, remembers your goals, and warns you before a "quick fix" makes things worse.**

**October 21, 2026.** Today the CreditCoach team launched CreditCoach, a chat assistant for young and first-time borrowers who want to understand their credit score and improve it steadily. Ask CreditCoach "why did my score drop?" and you get an answer based on your own score history and account balances, not a generic list of tips. CreditCoach remembers what you are working toward, such as a car or an apartment, and plans around it. It will never promise you a number, and it will tell you plainly when a product you're considering could hurt you.

For many people, the first year with a credit card is also the first time they see their score fall and have no idea why. The internet offers endless conflicting advice, most of it generic and some of it written to sell something. Worse, the moment a borrower panics about a score drop is exactly when payday lenders and "credit repair" services are most appealing, and most dangerous. First-time borrowers don't need more information. They need someone to explain *their* situation calmly and honestly.

CreditCoach does that in three ways. First, it looks at your data: it reads your score history and your card balances and limits, so when it says your utilization is 37.4%, that is your number, calculated from your accounts. Second, it explains what the data means, drawing on a curated library of credit-education content about what moves a score, how much, and for how long. Third, it remembers. Tell it once that you're saving for a car and want a 720 score by next year, and the next time you visit it builds its advice around that goal without being reminded.

"Every first-time borrower deserves a straight answer about their own credit, without being sold something," said the CreditCoach team. "We built CreditCoach to hold three lines no matter what. It never guarantees a score. It never recommends a predatory product. And it never states a figure it didn't read from your actual data. When it doesn't know something, it says so."

Using CreditCoach is as simple as sending a message. Ask "what's my utilization right now?" and CreditCoach shows each card's balance against its limit and your overall ratio. It also tells you which card is pulling your score down. Ask "I want to buy a car in 12 months, what should I focus on?" and you get a short, prioritized list of habits tied to your timeline: pay down the high-utilization card, hold off on new credit applications, and keep every payment on time. Ask whether you should take out a payday loan to clear your card, and CreditCoach explains clearly why that is high-risk. It then points you to safer options like a payment plan with your card issuer or free credit counselling.

"I watched my score drop 20 points and honestly panicked," said Aravind, 22, who opened their first credit card eight months ago. "I spent a whole night reading articles that contradicted each other, and one forum told me to take a payday loan to pay off my card. CreditCoach looked at my actual accounts and told me it was a hard inquiry plus one card being almost maxed out. It said both are common and usually temporary. It showed me exactly which card to pay down, and it talked me out of the payday loan in about two sentences. It didn't promise me I'd hit 720. It just gave me a plan I believe in, and it remembered my car goal the next time I logged in."

CreditCoach is available today as a web chat through a shareable link. It is free and requires no bank login. All guidance is educational, not financial advice. To get started, open CreditCoach and ask the question that's been worrying you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Customer FAQ

#### Getting started

**C1. What exactly can CreditCoach tell me?**
CreditCoach can explain why your score changed in a given month, show your current credit utilization (overall and per card), explain what each credit-score factor means and how much it typically matters, and help you build a prioritized plan toward a goal you set. Its explanations combine your own score and account data with a curated library of credit-education content, so you get "here's what happened to *you*," not a generic checklist.

**C2. Who is CreditCoach for?**
It's built for young and first-time borrowers: people in their first few years of work who have one or two credit cards and want to understand their score without wading through conflicting advice. Anyone can use it, but the explanations assume no prior knowledge of how credit works.

**C3. What do I need to get started?**
Just the link. There's no bank login and no sign-up form. In this version you're identified by a simulated user ID, and CreditCoach reads a sample credit profile rather than your real bank or bureau data.

**C4. Does it cost anything?**
No. CreditCoach is free to use, and it never earns money by pointing you to a lender or financial product.

**C5. Will using CreditCoach lower my credit score?**
No. CreditCoach only reads your score history and account data. It never applies for credit on your behalf, so it can't create a hard inquiry, which is the kind of check that can cause a small dip.

#### Understanding my score

**C6. How does CreditCoach work out why my score dropped?**
It does two things. First, it looks up your score history and the factor changes recorded for that month, such as a utilization spike or a new hard inquiry. Second, it retrieves the explanation of those factors from its reference library. The answer connects the two: what changed on your record, what that factor means, and how much it typically affects a score.

**C7. What is credit utilization, and how does CreditCoach calculate mine?**
Utilization is the share of your available revolving credit that you're currently using: your balance divided by your limit. CreditCoach calculates it for each card and across all your cards from your actual balances and limits. For example, ₹74,750 used across ₹2,00,000 of total card limits is 37.4%. Installment loans, like an education or car loan, have no limit and aren't part of this ratio. It never estimates this figure.

**C8. I pay my card in full every month. Why did my utilization still hurt my score?**
Utilization is usually based on the balance reported to the bureaus, which can be your balance around the statement date rather than after you pay. A large purchase that's reported before you pay it off can show up as a utilization spike, even if you never carry a balance. According to our reference guide, this is one of the most common causes of a short-term score dip, and its effect typically fades about one reporting cycle after the balance comes down.

**C9. How much does a hard inquiry hurt, and for how long?**
Our reference guide says a hard inquiry, which happens when you apply for new credit, typically lowers a score by about 2 to 10 points. It stays on your report for around two years, but its effect on your score usually fades within about 12 months. CreditCoach will show you whether an inquiry actually appears in your history for the month in question.

**C10. What happens if I miss a payment?**
Payment history is typically the single biggest factor in most scoring models. Our reference guide says a payment more than 30 days late can lower a score by roughly 60 to 110 points and can stay on a report for up to seven years, with its effect fading over about two years. If you're worried about missing a payment, CreditCoach can explain options like contacting your card issuer about a payment plan.

**C11. Should I close my oldest credit card?**
Usually, think twice. Closing your oldest card can shorten the average age of your accounts, which our reference guide says may lower your score by roughly 5 to 20 points. CreditCoach can walk you through this trade-off, but the decision is yours.

**C12. Should I take out a loan just to improve my credit mix?**
Generally, no. Credit mix is a minor factor, worth about 10% in most models, compared with payment history and utilization. Taking on debt you don't need, just to add an account type, usually isn't worth the cost or the new hard inquiry.

**C13. Where do the numbers CreditCoach quotes come from?**
Every personal figure CreditCoach states comes from a data lookup made while answering your question. That includes your score, balances, limits, and utilization. Every explanation of how a factor works comes from its reference library. If a lookup fails, CreditCoach won't guess. It tells you it can't pull your latest data right now and shares the last figures it confirmed, with a note on when they were confirmed.

**C14. What if CreditCoach can't reach my data?**
It says so plainly instead of guessing. You'll see something like "I can't pull your latest report right now. Here's what I last confirmed," along with the date those figures were confirmed. You can try again a moment later.

#### Goals and plans

**C15. Will CreditCoach tell me what my score will be in six months?**
No. Nobody can honestly promise that, and CreditCoach won't try. Scores are calculated by models we don't control, and they respond to things outside any plan, like a lender reporting late or an unexpected expense. What CreditCoach *will* do is tell you which habits are most associated with improvement, and share typical impact ranges from its reference content. For example, a utilization spike above 30% typically costs 10 to 40 points and fades about one reporting cycle after you pay it down. It always labels these as typical ranges, not predictions.

**C16. If I follow CreditCoach's plan, will I definitely hit my target score?**
CreditCoach can't guarantee that, and it will tell you so directly. Score changes depend on many things outside any plan. What it can do is help you focus on the habits most associated with steady improvement, such as keeping utilization low, paying on time, and avoiding unnecessary applications, and help you track how your score responds.

**C17. Does CreditCoach remember me? Can I change or remove my goal?**
Yes. If you tell CreditCoach your goal, for example "I'm saving for a car and want 720 by next year," it stores three things: your target score, your target date, and the purpose. It uses them in future sessions. It only changes your goal when you tell it to. You can update or clear your goal at any time by asking.

**C18. Can I have more than one goal?**
In this version, CreditCoach keeps one active goal at a time: a target score, a target date, and a purpose. If your priorities change, just tell it your new goal and it will replace the old one after confirming with you.

**C19. What if my goal isn't realistic?**
CreditCoach will be honest. If a target looks ambitious for the time available, it will say so and explain why, based on your current data. It won't quietly swap your goal for a different one. The goal stays yours, and CreditCoach plans around it.

**C20. I want to buy a car in 12 months. What kind of plan will I get?**
A short, prioritized list of habits tied to your timeline and your actual accounts. For example: pay down the card with the highest utilization first, hold off on new credit applications in the months before you apply for the car loan, and keep every payment on time. Each step is explained. None of it is framed as a guarantee.

#### Risky products and getting help

**C21. Should I take out a payday loan to pay off my credit card?**
CreditCoach will tell you clearly: no, that's high-risk. Payday loans and cash-advance products often carry annual percentage rates far higher than standard credit. Many payday lenders don't report to the major bureaus, so the loan doesn't help build your score, and a missed payment can end up in collections, which can seriously damage it. CreditCoach will suggest safer options, such as a payment plan with your card issuer, a balance transfer if you qualify, or free credit counselling.

**C22. Someone offered to "fix" my credit for a fee. Can CreditCoach help me decide?**
Yes. That's one of the things CreditCoach is built to do. It will tell you plainly about the red flags. A company that guarantees a specific point increase, or asks for payment before doing any work, is a warning sign under general consumer-protection guidance.

**C23. Where can I get real, personal help if I need it?**
In India, legitimate credit counselling is available free of charge, for example through bank-run financial literacy and credit counselling centres. If your situation goes beyond education, for example debt you can't manage, CreditCoach will point you there.

**C24. Will CreditCoach recommend a specific credit card or loan?**
No. CreditCoach doesn't recommend specific branded products, even good ones, because it can't judge how well a product fits your full financial picture. It will explain how product types work and what to look out for.

#### Trust and limits

**C25. Is CreditCoach financial advice?**
No. CreditCoach provides general education to help you understand your credit and make your own decisions. It does not provide personalized financial, legal, or tax advice, and it does not offer, arrange, or broker loans or credit repair.

**C26. Is my information safe?**
This version uses only simulated credit data, and you're identified by a simulated user ID, not your name. The only thing CreditCoach saves about you is your goal (target score, target date, and purpose) and a record of how each answer was produced, which helps us check its accuracy. You can clear your goal at any time.

**C27. What if CreditCoach gets something wrong, or can't help with my question?**
For anything important, check against your official credit report or your lender. CreditCoach focuses on a first-time borrower's core questions, so topics like home-loan planning, business credit, investing, and scoring systems outside India are out of scope. If you ask about one of these, it will tell you it can't help rather than guess.

### Internal FAQ

**I1. What data does CreditCoach handle, and how is it protected?** *(Data handling)*
For this build, CreditCoach connects to no real credit bureau, bank, or card issuer. All score history and account data is synthetic, generated for 13 fictional Indian user profiles (amounts in rupees) and stored as a static dataset in the repo. Users are identified by a simulated user ID, with no login and no real personal information. The only data CreditCoach writes is the goal memory (target score, target date, purpose) and observability logs, both stored locally in SQLite. Logs record tool calls, retrievals, and guardrail events under a trace ID. We do not add personal details beyond the synthetic user ID. The model is called through OpenRouter using a key kept in a git-ignored `.env` file. We never send real personal data to the model provider, because none exists in the system. If this ever moved toward real data, it would need consent, encryption, retention limits, and a review of provider data policies before launch. That is explicitly out of scope for this build. The shared demo link is protected by a single team login, set in the host's `.env` (`APP_USERNAME` / `APP_PASSWORD`) and shared privately. It exists to stop strangers using the demo and its model credits, and it is not a customer account.

**I2. How does CreditCoach avoid recommending predatory products?** *(Guardrails / predatory-product policy)*
We have a written policy, detailed in Week 3's `docs/guardrails.md`. CreditCoach never recommends or endorses payday loans, cash-advance products, guaranteed "credit repair" schemes, or advance-fee offers. When a user asks about one, CreditCoach flags it as high-risk *proactively*, even if the user didn't ask whether it was risky. The policy is enforced in three places. The system prompt states the rule. A guardrail layer checks every product-related answer before it reaches the user and reframes any response that reads as an endorsement. And the explanation of *why* a product is risky comes from our reference library, so it is grounded rather than improvised. Every refusal comes with a safer alternative, such as a payment plan, a balance transfer if the user qualifies, or free credit counselling.

**I3. How do we avoid over-blocking legitimate questions?**
We draw a clear line between *explaining* a product, which is allowed and useful ("what is a payday loan?", "how is a payday loan different from a credit card?"), and *endorsing* it, which is refused. The guardrail classifier is prompted with that distinction. The eval suite includes a set of benign questions that must not be refused, so over-blocking shows up as a failing test just like under-blocking does.

**I4. How do we stop the model from guaranteeing outcomes or inventing figures?**
Both are handled by rule plus check. For guarantees, the system prompt forbids promising any score or timeline, and the guardrail layer scans draft responses for guarantee language ("you will reach," "guaranteed," "definitely by") and reframes them around habits. For fabricated figures, tool results are passed to the model in structured form, and a provenance check compares every number in the draft response against that request's tool output. A number that doesn't match is removed or the answer is regenerated. Both checks are covered by automated eval cases, including indirect rephrasings, so a regression shows up as a failing test.

**I5. Why use RAG instead of relying on the model's own knowledge?**
A model's built-in knowledge of credit scoring is broad but unsourced, may be out of date, and can drift toward confident generalizations. Retrieving from a curated library means every factor explanation and impact range comes from content we chose and reviewed, starting with `credit_score_factors_guide.pdf`. It also means we can test retrieval on its own (Task #9) and fix wrong answers by fixing the corpus, not by re-prompting.

**I6. Which models do we use, and why go through OpenRouter?**
GPT-5 is the main chat model. GPT-5 mini or GPT-4.1 mini handles cheaper side calls such as guardrail classification and eval judging. GPT-4o is the fallback if GPT-5 is slow or unavailable. OpenRouter gives us all of these through one API key and one OpenAI-compatible client, so switching or comparing models is a config change, not a code change. That also supports the stretch goal of hitting a latency and cost budget.

**I7. How is the synthetic dataset built, and why 13 profiles?**
Task #6 built 13 synthetic users in an Indian context: rupee amounts, scores on the 300–900 range of Indian credit bureaus, and Indian loan types. USR-001 is Aravind, from the sample data. USR-002 to USR-013 are one profile per person we interviewed: their cards, card usage, loans, savings and goals come from that person's answers, while names, balances and scores are generated to fit. Every monthly score change is checked against the factors guide's ranges. The profiles cover a utilization spike, hard inquiries, a late payment, high utilization with an instant loan app, a loan-only file, two users with no credit file, and steady improvers. See `data/README.md`.

**I8. How does goal memory work, and how do we stop it overriding the user?**
Memory stores one record per user: target score, target date, and purpose, in a local SQLite store. The goal is written only when the user explicitly states or changes it, and every write is logged. On each new session, the stored goal is loaded into context so CreditCoach can reference it unprompted. The agent may question whether a goal is realistic, but it cannot rewrite the goal on its own initiative. Cross-session recall is tested in Task #17.

**I9. What happens when a tool or the model provider fails during a conversation?**
CreditCoach degrades gracefully instead of guessing. If the score or account tool times out, it tells the user it can't pull the latest data and shares the last confirmed figures with their timestamp. If GPT-5 is slow or unavailable through OpenRouter, requests fall back to GPT-4o. Every failure and fallback is logged under the request's trace ID and counted on the observability dashboard, so we can see the tool-failure rate and how the agent handled each failure.

**I10. How will we know CreditCoach works?**
We turn the six sample queries from requirements.md into an automated eval suite with pass/fail scoring. We record a baseline, do error analysis on the failures, fix the top issues, and re-run to show the improvement. Our targets are: 6 of 6 queries passing, zero fabricated figures, correct refusal on every payday-loan and guarantee test with no false blocks on benign questions, correct goal recall across sessions, and a graceful fallback on every simulated tool timeout. The full metrics are in section 8 of the 6-pager.

**I11. What will we have at the end of each week?**
Week 1: a Gradio chat that answers "why did my score drop?" from the reference library, with a shareable link, plus this PR/FAQ and the 6-pager. Week 2: live score and account data through MCP tools, and goal memory across two sessions, visible in an agent-trace panel. Week 3: guardrails that refuse payday-loan endorsements and guarantees, and caching with a visible cache-hit badge. Week 4: observability dashboard, eval scores before and after error analysis, and a rehearsed live demo with a backup video.

**I12. What are we deliberately not building?**
We are not building real bureau or bank integrations, user accounts or authentication, score predictions, branded product recommendations, loan origination, or credit repair. Topics are limited to a first-time borrower's core questions, so home-loan planning, business credit, investing, and scoring systems outside India are out of scope. Our users are Indian consumers, and scores use the 300–900 range of Indian credit bureaus. These limits keep the build achievable in four weeks and keep the product clearly on the education side of the line.
