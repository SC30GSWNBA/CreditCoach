High-level design

How CreditCoach answers one question

CreditCoach is a chat assistant that explains your credit score using your own account data and a library of credit lessons. This board follows a single question from your keyboard to a checked, sourced answer. Each row is one part of the system; read left to right and follow the numbers.

The fine print, row by row

Guardrails: the nine rules

RuleCreditCoach mustIf broken
G1Never promise or predict a score, a gain or a dateRewrite
G2Never endorse a predatory product; flag it as high-risk and offer a safer optionRewrite
G3Never invent a figure; every number comes from this turn's tools or the libraryRewrite, else block
G4Never change your saved goal without your say-soThe save is refused
G5Record tool failures and say so plainly, never estimateFallback answer
S1Keep its rules when a message says to ignore themDecline, then help
S2Never reveal its hidden prompt (14 copied words in a row is a leak)Decline
S3Never keep or repeat personal identifiersMask
S4Only ever discuss the signed-in user's dataDecline
  • Three layers enforce them: the system prompt tells the model the rules, the tools and hosts make some breaks impossible, and the guardrail layer checks every question and every answer.
  • Eight rails run in total: three on the question, five on the answer. Seven are fixed pattern and number checks (under 0.1 s together); one is a small-model wording review.
  • A rewrite adds 5 to 20 s because the main model writes again. You never see the rejected draft.
  • Every decision is one JSON log line, and it drives the badge above the answer: passed, high-risk product not endorsed, no guarantee given, rewritten, or blocked.
  • Red-teamed with 28 adversarial prompts in seven attack types plus 4 harmless controls. Final live run: 28 of 28 held, 4 of 4 controls untouched.

Cache: four layers in Redis

LayerWhat is reusedKept forSaves
EmbeddingA question's 384-number vector7 days~5 ms
RetrievalThe passages found for a question24 hours~20 ms
ToolYour score-history or account lookup5 minutes~1.8 s
AnswerA finished, safety-checked answer1 hour~11 s + one GPT-5 call
  • "Same question" is decided in three steps: exact match; same meaningful words with similarity of at least 0.90; or a rewording at least 0.80 similar that GPT-5 mini confirms.
  • Before a stored answer is served, your figures are read again and compared. If they changed, the stored answer is deleted and a fresh one is written.
  • Measured: 11.05 s uncached against 0.009 s cached (median), and $0.0122 of model cost against none.
  • Keys include your user ID, so one user can never read another's entries.
  • No Redis configured? The cache lives in the app's own memory (5,000 entries, lost on restart). Redis down? Every lookup counts as a miss and Redis is left alone for 30 s.

RAG pipeline: how the library is searched

  • Built ahead of time. 17 Markdown documents are split at paragraphs, lists and tables into 45 chunks of at most 240 tokens. Each chunk starts with its document title and is stored in ChromaDB with its vector, category and source.
  • At question time. Normalise bureau names, embed, take the 12 nearest chunks by cosine similarity, rerank with a cross-encoder, keep at most 2 per document, return the top 3.
  • Searches from your own data (Task 30). When your history explains the question, the library is searched for that too: the factor behind a month you name, the factors behind your last two falls, or the interest on a card balance you carry. At most 2 passages are added; most questions get none.
  • Three shelves. Scoring factors (18 chunks), money basics (16), product risks (11). A question about a risky product always gets 2 extra passages from the product-risk shelf.
  • Cited. The model must cite passages as [1], [2], [3], and the chat lists them under every answer.
  • Not in the library? The model says "I don't have specific information on that" and sticks to general principles.

Tools and MCP: where your numbers come from

  • MCP (Model Context Protocol) is the standard plug between an AI and its tools. The host starts the CreditCoach MCP server as a small side process and talks to it over stdio.
  • get_score_history(period) accepts latest, last_N_months (1 to 24), YYYY-MM, YYYY-MM:YYYY-MM or all.
  • get_account_summary() returns every account with balance, limit and utilization, plus totals.
  • The model is shown the tools without a user-ID field. The host adds it from your sign-in.
  • Amounts are whole rupees. Ratios are decimals to three places (0.374 means 37.4%).
  • Error codes: INVALID_PERIOD, PERIOD_OUT_OF_RANGE, UNKNOWN_USER, USER_MISMATCH, DATA_UNAVAILABLE (retried once), UNKNOWN_TOOL, INVALID_ARGUMENTS. An error never carries partial data, and the model must not fill the gap.

Memory: three kinds, plus dreaming

KindHoldsWritten by
EpisodicWhat happened, in order: sign-ins, questions, replies, goal changes, errors, sign-outsThe chat, live
SemanticYour goal (target score, target date, purpose) and facts you sharedGoal: only you. Facts: dreaming
ProceduralHow you like to be helped, such as "keep answers short"Dreaming
  • Dreaming runs in the background at sign-in. It merges duplicates, drops stale items, summarises each session, and keeps at most 20 facts and 10 preferences.
  • Dreaming may only suggest a goal. The chat asks you to confirm before anything is saved.
  • A goal needs a score of 300 to 900, a date as YYYY-MM or YYYY, or a purpose of up to 200 characters.
  • Memory never holds a credit figure. Figures always come live from the tools.

LLMs: who writes what

ModelJobRuns
GPT-5Writes every fresh answer and any rewriteCloud, via OpenRouter
GPT-5 miniDreaming, "same question?" for the cache, the wording reviewCloud, via OpenRouter

  • One OpenRouter key covers every cloud model. Each call is capped at 16,000 output tokens and 90 seconds.
  • The model may ask for tools in up to 4 rounds. On the next turn no tools are offered, so it has to answer.
  • An answer written by the backup model is shown but never cached.

Observability: one trace per question

Step on the boardRecorded as
The whole questionOne trace: user, session, question, answer, total time
Guardrail checksA span each way, and a "trigger" event per rule that fired
CacheAn event for every lookup: hit, miss, store or skip
RAG pipelineA span with the passages found and their scores
Tools + MCPA span per call; a failure is marked as an error, with its code
LLMsA span per call: model, tokens and cost
  • Built with OpenTelemetry. Every trace is written to local files (.traces/) and, when its keys are set, sent to Langfuse Cloud.
  • The trace ID is saved with each reply in memory, so any answer can be traced back step by step.
  • Questions are recorded only after personal identifiers are masked. Recording can never break an answer.
  • A Dashboard tab, for every signed-in user, reads the traces back (Task 31): tool-call failure rate, guardrail triggers per rule, and goal-recall accuracy. Real use is what the chat app answered (traces tagged app); eval and test runs (eval:<label>, script:<name>) are kept apart and listed by run.

Evaluation: one command, four scorers

ScorerA case passes it when
ExpectedEvery separate thing requirements.md expects of the question holds, checked automatically
FiguresEvery number in the answer traces to the tools or the library
JudgeAn independent small model says it does what is expected, is grounded and is clear
RunNo crash, the main model answered, and the guardrail layer didn't block it
  • python -m creditcoach.evals runs the 6 sample questions the whole way through the board above; --all adds the other 44.
  • Each case is one trace, and its scores are attached to that trace in Langfuse, so a failing case opens onto the steps that explain it.
  • Runs skip the cache and use throwaway memory, so they measure the agent and touch no one's history.
  • Baseline (Task 28): 36 of 50 cases pass. After the Task 30 fixes: 45 of 50. --compare writes the before/after report, with cases whose measurement changed counted apart.

GPT-5GPT-4o answers once, and the fallback is logged
A tool call5 s timeout, one retry, then "I can't pull your latest data right now". No estimate. If the same lookup worked earlier in the conversation, that figure is shared, labelled as last confirmed and when. A sign-up's question is handed back
The tool serverCan't start, hangs at startup (20 s) or dies mid-call: every lookup reports the data as unavailable, and the answer goes on
The library or the user's profileThe answer goes on without it, and says it has no library information

All user data in CreditCoach is synthetic. Its guidance is educational, not financial advice. Amounts are in ₹ and scores use the 300–900 range of Indian credit bureaus.
